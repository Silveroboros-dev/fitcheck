## Recording cues

Keep the interface in English and preserve the top-left logo. Leave a short
pause after “weak proxy” and after “no clean expression.”

Show the complete accepted criterion before introducing labels. Keep the
recording focused on the thesis, contracts, relationship and mismatches;
technical IDs and operational provenance are supporting detail.

The packaged curated case uses project-authored summaries of the retained
contract conditions. Call those summaries, not verbatim original rules.
Its earlier-result view reconstructs the prior labels for explanation.
The exact original private run remains preserved separately. When recording
the local private replay instead, its original-run control shows the actual
saved packet. Use the wording appropriate to the displayed version.

Do not perform acceptance or market-selection clicks on a read-only replay.
Describe the criterion as a choice made for this example, not a decision just
made by the visitor on camera.

The weak-proxy label is an attributable proposal, not a named human semantic
adjudication. Automatic relatedness-proposal generation for arbitrary new
markets is not implemented. Neither this case nor its code checks establish
general classifier accuracy.

This recording demonstrates thesis-to-contract fit. It does not demonstrate
continuous monitoring, a causal effect, an automatic probability update,
trading, or the arrival of AGI. Keep those out of present-tense product claims.

## Short explanation to memorize

“RSI is related because its criteria explicitly involve autonomous AI research.
It is a weak proxy because it resolves on a different event, for different
companies and an earlier deadline. Either contract outcome can leave our
2028 thesis unsettled.”

If asked how this connects to causal inference:

“Before using a signal in a decision, I want to know what it measures and what
assumptions connect it to my belief. FitCheck makes that relationship
inspectable. Estimating the effect of an intervention is a further question.”
